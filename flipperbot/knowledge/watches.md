# Watches: what moves the price, what to look for, what to avoid

Knowledge for flipping second-hand watches bought in Romania (OLX, Facebook, Okazii) and resold locally
(OLX, Okazii) or abroad (Chrono24, eBay, Catawiki). Written for the bot's AI checks and for a human
reading along. Prices are relative, not quotes: always check sold prices for the exact reference.

## Cheat sheet (the short version the quick AI read gets)

- Value = brand x reference x ORIGINALITY x condition x completeness. Originality beats age and shine.
- Mechanical > quartz for resale (quartz loses value fast, even from good brands; tuning-fork Accutron is the exception).
- Original, untouched dial is the biggest single factor. Redials, relumes, replaced hands, "service dials" cut value hard.
- Over-polished cases (soft edges, blurred bevels) lose value. Never polish to flip.
- Box, papers, receipts, service records add 10-25% on modern Swiss/luxury; little on cheap or very old pieces.
- Cheap luxury (Rolex, Omega, AP, Hublot, Patek, Cartier, Tag) on OLX is usually a replica: start from "fake".
  Popular Seikos are faked too (Presage Cocktail Time, SKX, "Seiko mods" sold as originals): under a third of the
  real price = replica until caseback text, case code, calibre, jewel count and the exact reference all check out.
- Sleepers sellers don't recognise: Universal Geneve, Zodiac, Eberhard, Vulcain (Cricket), Movado (vintage), Enicar,
  Doxa, Eterna, Cyma, Juvenia, Nivada, Excelsior Park, Minerva, Angelus, Gallet, Wittnauer, Heuer (pre-TAG),
  early Seiko automatics (6139, 6105, 62MAS, King/Grand Seiko, Lord Marvel), Soviet Strela/3017, Okean, early Sturmanskie,
  Poljot 3133 chronographs, solid-gold cases (585/750), chronographs with Valjoux/Lemania/Venus/Landeron movements.
- Red flags: "replica/AAA/1:1/clone", factory names (noob, VSF, clean), Japanese/Chinese movement in a Swiss brand,
  "many models available / gift box / last pieces", stock photos, ticking seconds on a "Rolex", see-through caseback on Rolex,
  Apple Watch "Ultra" at 300 lei (HK9/DT8/T800 clones), iCloud-locked smartwatches.
- Fast sellers: Seiko 5/SKX/Presage/Prospex, Orient Bambino/Kamasu, G-Shock GA-2100/5600, Tissot PRX, Hamilton Khaki,
  Longines HydroConquest, Omega Seamaster/Speedmaster, Apple Watch (recent), Garmin Fenix/Forerunner.
- Slow sellers: fashion brands (MK, Guess, Fossil, DW, Armani), generic "lux" watches, ladies' quartz, worn plated gold,
  odd sizes, modded Seikos (to collectors), anything over-polished or redialed.

## What moves the price

1. **Brand and reference.** The exact reference (Seiko 6139-6002, Omega 166.0202, Rolex 16610) sets the band.
   Model family names ("Seamaster", "Constellation", "Seiko 5") span cheap to expensive: never price by family alone.
2. **Originality.** Two watches of the same model can differ by multiples. Collectors check that dial, hands,
   bezel insert, crown, case, caseback, bracelet/end links and movement all belong together and to the era.
   - *Redial*: a refinished dial (new paint/print). Tells: fuzzy or too-crisp print, wrong font, misaligned text,
     missing "Swiss Made"/"T Swiss T" where it should be, dial feet or markers moved, lume that doesn't match the hands.
   - *Franken*: parts from different watches assembled to look like a rarer model. Tells: caseback reference vs
     movement calibre mismatch, movement serial far from case era, generic crown, wrong hands.
   - *Service parts*: replacement dials/hands/inserts from the maker are legitimate but worth less than originals.
3. **Condition.** Case: sharp lines and bevels beat shiny-but-polished. Crystal: acrylic scratches polish out
   cheaply; sapphire chips cost more. Dial: patina is fine, water damage/rot is not. Movement: running and keeping
   time; a full service is a cost (see below), not a value add for most buyers.
4. **Completeness.** Box & papers (certificate, warranty card, receipt), extra links, original bracelet/strap.
5. **Movement type.** Mechanical/automatic sells; quartz from the 1980s-2000s is hard to sell above parts value
   unless it's a cult piece (Omega Seamaster quartz is liquid, Accutron tuning fork is collectible, Seiko Twin Quartz,
   Grand Seiko 9F, Citizen Chronomaster).
6. **Complications and materials.** Chronographs (especially column-wheel/Valjoux 72/7733/7750, Lemania 1873,
   Venus 175/188, Landeron 48/51, Poljot 3133/3017) beat time-only. Solid gold (14k = 585, 18k = 750) has a metal floor;
   gold-plated/filled ("placat", "dublé", "gold filled", "RGP", "20 microns") does not.
7. **Size and taste.** 36-42 mm sells best for men; under 34 mm men's vintage sells slower except as "unisex".
   Ladies' watches sell slower and cheaper unless luxury.

## Sleeper brands and pieces: valuable but not household names

Vintage Swiss (1940s-1970s mechanicals especially):
- **Universal Geneve**: Polerouter, Tri-Compax/Compax chronographs, White Shadow. Historically important, still undervalued.
- **Zodiac**: Sea Wolf divers, Aerospace GMT, triple-date chronographs (near-twins of Heuer Carrera).
- **Eberhard**: Scafograf divers, chronographs; scarce and wanted.
- **Vulcain**: Cricket alarm watch (a bargain dealers recommend), Valjoux 7733 chronographs.
- **Movado**: vintage mechanicals (not the modern quartz Museum, which is cheap), Kingmatic, chronographs.
- **Enicar** (Sherpa divers, Sherpa Graph), **Doxa** (Sub), **Eterna** (KonTiki, Eterna-matic), **Cyma** (Watersport),
  **Juvenia**, **Nivada** (Chronomaster), **Excelsior Park** (chronograph movements), **Minerva**, **Angelus** (Chronodato),
  **Gallet** (Multichron), **Wakmann**, **Wittnauer**, **Election**, **West End**, **Girard-Perregaux** (vintage Gyromatic),
  **Ulysse Nardin** (vintage chronometers), **Heuer** (pre-1985, Autavia/Carrera/Camaro/Monaco).
- Early **Omega** three-hand Seamasters, Constellation "pie-pan", Flightmaster, Speedmaster (cal. 321/861), Dynamic.
- **Longines** 30CH/13ZN chronographs, Conquest, Flagship; **Tissot** Navigator, PR516, Visodate, Seastar.
- **Zenith** El Primero, A386, vintage Defy.

Japan:
- **Seiko**: 6139 (Pogue/Bullhead/Helmet chronographs), 6138, 6105 (Willard), 6309/6306 (Turtle), 62MAS 6217,
  King Seiko, Grand Seiko, Lord Marvel, LordMatic, Bellmatic alarm, 7A28/7A38 Giugiaro chronographs, "Arnie" H558.
  Seiko never used radioactive lume on domestic pieces: "radium" on a Seiko is a story.
- **Citizen**: Challenge Timer, Bullhead, Promaster, Chronomaster; **Orient** King Diver, Grand Prix.

Soviet and Eastern Europe:
- **Poljot 3017 "Strela"** (Leonov's spacewalk chronograph) is the top Soviet piece; **early Sturmanskie** (Gagarin era,
  1MChZ), **Okean** (navy chronograph), **Poljot 3133** chronographs, Poljot gold "President" pieces.
- **Vostok** Precision, early Amphibia (radio-room, "Neptune"), Komandirskie "generalskie"; **Raketa** Copernicus, Big Zero,
  24-hour polar; **Slava** Super-automatic; **Pobeda** (common, cheap) and **ZIM**.
- Fakes and frankens are common at the top end (Strela especially): demand clear movement and caseback photos.
- East German **Glashütte (GUB)** Spezimatic, **Ruhla** (cheap).

Pocket watches:
- Solid gold (585/750, 14K/18K, Swiss 0.585) and silver (800/900/925/935) cases have a metal floor; gold-filled ("GF",
  "RGP", "Warranted 20 years") and silver-plated don't. Swiss quality makers (Longines, Omega, Zenith, IWC, Patek,
  Vacheron, Ulysse Nardin) and complications (chronograph, repeater, calendar) carry most value; US railroad grades are
  modest unless solid gold.

## Fakes: how to tell, by brand

The checklist (any clear fail = fake; a real watch passes all of them):
1. **Text, letter by letter**: dial, caseback, rotor, clasp. Replicas misspell ("MADW IN JAPAN") and get fonts wrong.
2. **Codes belong together**: the caseback/case code, the movement calibre and the model reference must match the
   maker's records (Seiko case 4R35-01T0 = a closed-dial date watch, not an open heart; Omega caseback reference vs calibre).
3. **Movement**: jewel count and rotor text for that calibre (Seiko 4R35/4R36: 23-24 jewels; "21 JEWELS" on a 4R
   rotor is wrong); generic or Chinese bridges through an open heart or display back.
4. **The configuration exists**: find the real reference with this exact dial colour, case, bracelet/strap and size.
   Replicas invent combinations (e.g. a black-dial Presage Cocktail Time open heart on a bracelet: the real faceted-index
   open heart, SSA405J1, is dark blue on leather). Seiko prints a calibre-dial code next to "MADE IN JAPAN" at 6.
5. **Size**: stated diameter vs the real one (Cocktail Time 40.5 mm, not 43).
6. **Packaging and seller**: cheap white boxes with thick misaligned logos, bracelets wrapped in crinkly film, the same
   watch listed many times, stock photos.
7. **Price**: under about a third of what a real one sells for is a replica until every point above passes.

General:
- Ticking seconds hand on a watch that should sweep (Rolex, Omega automatic, most Swiss automatics) = quartz fake.
- Light weight, wobbly or blurry printing, crooked date window, weak date magnifier, logo shape wrong.
- Movement: a Miyota/Seagull/Chinese generic in a Swiss brand, or a display caseback the real model never had.
- Listing patterns: one seller with many luxury watches far under market; stock or catalogue photos; "AAA", "1:1",
  "clone", "superclone", factory names (noob, VSF, clean, ZF, APF, AR, BT); "gift box", "many models", "last pieces".
Rolex: solid (no display) caseback; date magnified ~2.5x by the cyclops; rehaut engraving crisp and aligned with the
crown at 12 (2005+); laser-etched crown in the crystal at 6 (modern); serial between lugs/on rehaut matches papers.
Omega: Futura-style logo crisp and correctly sized; Seamaster wave dial and caseback medallion detail; vintage serial
on the movement, not on a lug; movement calibre must match the case reference (165.024 -> cal. 552, 166.024 -> cal. 565);
vintage movements copper-plated; pie-pan Constellations are heavily faked and redialed.
Seiko vintage: caseback code, movement code and serial must agree (franken check from photos); aftermarket 6139 dials
show deep concentric rings in the subdial at 6 and coarse sunburst; 6105-811x original crown has six tooth groups
(fakes five); aftermarket dials/hands/inserts are common, ask what's original.
Smartwatches: Apple Watch "Ultra"/"Series 10" at a few hundred lei = clone (HK9, DT8, T800, "Hello Watch"); check
iCloud activation lock is off, battery health, exact model/size/GPS vs cellular.

## Reselling tips

- Buy the reference, not the story: confirm reference and calibre from caseback/movement photos before paying.
- Price against SOLD prices for the same reference and condition; asking prices on OLX run high.
- Costs to budget (Romania, roughly): battery 20-50 lei; acrylic crystal polish/replacement 50-150; strap 50-200;
  basic service of a simple mechanical 250-500; vintage Swiss chronograph service 800-1,500+; parts extra.
- Don't polish, don't swap the dial, keep old parts and receipts: originality is the value.
- Good photos sell: daylight, dial straight-on, caseback, movement if possible, crown, clasp, any flaws, wrist shot.
  State the reference, size, service history, and what's not original.
- Where: OLX (fast, local, haggling), Okazii (auctions), Facebook groups (vintage/Soviet), Chrono24/eBay/Catawiki
  for higher-value pieces (fees ~6.5% Chrono24, ~13% eBay; shipping and customs outside the EU).
- Liquidity matters as much as margin: a fast-selling Seiko at +30% beats a slow Swiss at +60% that sits for months.
- Negotiation room: listings up for weeks, recent price cuts, "negociabil", sellers clearing an estate.
