# Power tools: what moves the price, what to look for, what to avoid

Flipping second-hand power tools bought in Romania. Pro brands resell fast; DIY brands sell slowly and cheaply.

## Cheat sheet (the short version the quick AI read gets)

- Pro brands (Makita, DeWalt, Milwaukee, Hilti, Festool, Metabo, Bosch Professional blue) resell well; DIY (Bosch green,
  Einhell, Parkside, Ryobi, Black+Decker) sells slowly and cheaply.
- Kit vs bare tool: batteries and charger can be half the value. "Doar corp / fara acumulator / solo" = bare tool.
  Original batteries alone sell well (Makita/Milwaukee 5-6Ah).
- Brushless (BL, FUEL, XR brushless) and current platforms (Makita LXT 18V / XGT 40V, Milwaukee M18/M12, DeWalt 18V XR)
  are worth more than old NiCd/14.4V tools.
- Fakes: "Makita 48V / 88V / 199V" and other voltages the brands don't make are Chinese clones in brand colours; also
  "tip Makita" / "compatibil Makita". Cheap "Hilti" with no model code is suspect.
- Check: runs (video), worn carbon brushes, chuck play, cracked housing, battery health, the model code on the label.
- Fast sellers: impact drivers, drill/drivers, SDS rotary hammers (Hilti TE, Makita HR), angle grinders, mitre saws.
- Lots and estate sales ("lichidare atelier", "mostenire") hide good tools priced by weight.
