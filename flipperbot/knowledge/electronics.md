# Consoles, PCs, laptops and PC parts: what moves the price, what to look for, what to avoid

Knowledge for flipping second-hand consoles, handhelds, gaming PCs, laptops and PC parts bought in Romania
(OLX, Facebook) and resold locally. Prices move with releases and shortages: always price against what the same
model and variant sells for now (OLX sold-out listings, eBay sold, eMAG Resigilate for the new-ish ceiling).

## Cheat sheet (the short version the quick AI read gets)

- Price by EXACT model and variant: PS5 disc vs digital vs Pro, storage; Switch V1/V2/OLED/Lite/Switch 2; Steam Deck
  LCD vs OLED; RTX 3060 12 GB vs 8 GB, Ti/Super; laptop CPU+GPU generation. Brand alone says little.
- Gaming PCs and gaming laptops are worth roughly their graphics card plus the rest at used-part prices, minus
  10-20%. A PC with an RTX 4070 is a different product from one with a GTX 1650, whatever the case looks like.
- Fast sellers: PS5 (all versions), Switch OLED/Lite/Switch 2, Steam Deck, Xbox Series X/S, RTX 3060-4070,
  RX 6700 XT-7800 XT, Ryzen 5/7 AM4/AM5, MacBook Air M1/M2/M3, gaming laptops with RTX 3060+.
- Slow sellers: office laptops with old Intel (gen 7 and older), GTX 10-series and older, Xbox One, PS4 (falling),
  prebuilt PCs with unknown power supplies, anything with a password or lock, Intel MacBooks.
- Hype moves prices: GTA 6 (19 Nov 2026) pushed PS5/PS5 Pro and Xbox Series up; the AI memory shortage pushed
  RAM and graphics cards up. These can fall after launches and restocks: sell, don't hold.
- Red flags: far under market for a popular console (scam or bait), stock photos, "only transfer/courier", iCloud or
  MDM locked Macs (near worthless), BIOS password, banned console, ex-mining cards sold as "gaming only", reflowed
  GPUs, "nu porneste / pentru piese" (often not worth fixing), controllers with drift.
- Bundles: extra controllers and games add value but sell for less than their sum; a console "+ 10 jocuri" is
  priced mostly on the console.
- Retro (NES, SNES, N64, GameCube, Game Boy, PS1/PS2, Sega): complete in box and working is what collectors pay for;
  loose and untested sells cheap. Modded retro handhelds can sell well to players.

## What moves the price

1. **Model and variant.** Revisions and storage matter: PS5 slim vs original, 825 GB vs 1-2 TB; Switch V2 (better
   battery) and OLED vs V1; Steam Deck 64/256/512 GB/1 TB; GPU VRAM.
2. **Working and tested.** A photo or video of it running, games loading, a benchmark screenshot for a GPU or PC.
   "Untested" is priced as broken.
3. **Locks and accounts.** Activation Lock/MDM, BIOS passwords, console bans, school/company laptops still enrolled.
4. **Condition.** Screen (dead pixels, burn-in on OLED), battery health (laptops, handhelds), fan noise, drift,
   cosmetic wear, missing original charger or stand.
5. **Completeness.** Box, receipt (proves it's not stolen and shows warranty), original controller, charger.
6. **Timing.** New models and price rises (Sony/Nintendo raised prices in 2025-2026) lift used prices; a successor
   or a restock lowers them. Black Friday floods the market with new deals.

## Fakes, scams and traps

- Bait listings: a PS5 Pro at half price, asking for payment by transfer or "courier with payment at delivery" from a
  new account. Meet in person, test before paying.
- Mining cards: many identical GPUs from one seller; check fan wear and ask; not always bad, but price lower.
- "Gaming PC" with a weak or unbranded power supply, or a GPU name in the title that isn't in the photos.
- Handheld clones and fake controllers in bundles; refurbished consoles sold as new.
- Stolen goods: no box, no receipt, many identical new items, price far under market.

## Reselling tips

- Test everything in the photos: console on with the system info screen, laptop with the specs screen, GPU in a
  benchmark. Reset to factory and remove accounts before selling.
- Clean, photograph in daylight, list the exact model and specs in the title (that's what buyers search).
- Sell PC parts separately when the parts are worth more than the whole; sell bundles of games separately.
- Where: OLX (fast, local), Facebook groups (PC building, console trading), eMAG Marketplace for newer items.
