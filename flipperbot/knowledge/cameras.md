# Cameras and lenses: what moves the price, what to look for, what to avoid

Knowledge for flipping second-hand cameras and lenses bought in Romania and resold locally or abroad
(eBay, Kamerastore, Catawiki). Prices are relative; check sold prices for the exact item.

## Cheat sheet (the short version the quick AI read gets)

- Film is in demand: premium compacts (Contax T2/T3, Olympus mju II, Yashica T4/T5, Konica Hexar, Ricoh GR1),
  medium format (Pentax 67, Mamiya 645/7/RB67, Hasselblad 500, Rolleiflex TLRs), and classic SLRs (Nikon FM2/FE2/F3,
  Canon AE-1/A-1/F-1, Olympus OM-1/OM-2, Pentax MX/LX/Spotmatic) have risen a lot in recent years.
- Valuable vintage lenses: Zeiss Jena Biotar 58/2, Flektogon 35/2.4 and 20/2.8, Pancolar 50/1.8 (zebra);
  Meyer-Optik Trioplan 100/2.8 and Primoplan; Helios 40-2 85/1.5; Jupiter 3; any Leica/Leitz glass (Summicron,
  Summilux, Elmarit); Canon FD L, Nikon AI-S fast primes; Takumar 50/1.4. Common and cheap: Helios 44-2, Industar 50,
  Pentacon 50/1.8, kit zooms, Zenit E bodies, Smena, Lubitel.
- Condition decides: fungus, haze, separation, scratches on front/rear element, oily or stuck aperture blades, stiff
  focus, shutter not firing at all speeds, light seals, dead meter. "Tested with film" and "all speeds OK" add value.
- Fakes: Soviet FED/Zorki bodies engraved "Leica"/"Leitz" (often with eagles/Wehrmacht marks) are the classic fake;
  rebadged or repainted bodies sold as rarer models. A cheap "Leica" is almost always a FED/Zorki.
- Low resale: digital point-and-shoots (except trendy Ricoh GR/Fujifilm X100/Canon G-series/old CCD compacts),
  entry DSLRs with kit lenses, generic zooms, accessories, anything with fungus.
- Red flags: "nu stiu daca merge" with no shutter test, photos that never show the glass, "functional" with no proof,
  sellers listing many identical "rare" items.

## What moves the price

1. **Model and version.** Exact model and lens version matter (Helios 44-2 vs 44M-4; Flektogon 35/2.4 vs 35/2.8;
   multicoated "MC"/"zebra" versions; Leica M3 vs M2; Canon FD vs FD "new").
2. **Optical condition.** Look through the glass in the photos: fungus (threads/web), haze (milky), separation
   (rainbow edges), cleaning marks, scratches. Fungus can come back; cleaning costs money and may not fix etching.
3. **Mechanical condition.** Aperture blades dry and snappy (oil = service), focus smooth, shutter fires at all speeds
   (slow speeds sticking is common), light seals (cheap to replace), meter working (matters for auto cameras).
4. **Completeness.** Caps, hoods, original boxes, manuals add a little; for premium compacts a box matters more.
5. **Trend.** Point-and-shoot film compacts and medium format rose sharply; some forum voices think the peak passed:
   price against recent SOLD listings, not 2021 prices.

## Fakes and traps

- Fake Leica: FED/Zorki/Kiev with Leica/Leitz engravings, "Luftwaffe/Kriegsmarine" markings, odd serials; top plate
  font and shutter speed dial differ from real Leica screw-mount bodies (IIIf, IIIc). Real Leica serials can be checked.
- Rebadged Zenit/Kiev sold as Contax/Nikon, Kiev 88 sold as Hasselblad (similar look, much cheaper).
- "Untested" or "pentru piese" (for parts) on shutters and electronics: price as broken.
- Lens mount adapters, filters, hoods and bags sold with misleading "Leica/Zeiss" titles.

## Reselling tips

- Test what you can: shutter at all speeds, aperture, focus, meter; shine a light through the lens; run a roll for
  premium film bodies and say so.
- Photos: front and rear element with a light behind, aperture blades, mount, top plate, serial, any flaws.
- Clean and CLA (clean-lube-adjust) only where it pays: premium bodies and lenses, not Zenits.
- Where: OLX/Facebook groups locally; eBay and Kamerastore/KEH-level buyers for good glass; sell M42 lenses
  individually rather than as a lot.
- Liquidity: popular mounts (M42, Nikon F, Canon FD/EF, Leica M/M39, Sony E adapters) sell faster than odd mounts.
